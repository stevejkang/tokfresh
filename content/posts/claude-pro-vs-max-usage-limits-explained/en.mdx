> **Updated November 2026.** The Max 20x plan is now called **Ultra**: it has no 5-hour limit for verified accounts and 10x Pro usage per week. The separate weekly model cap now applies to **Fable**, not Opus. Weekly limits reset at a fixed time assigned to your account, not on a rolling 7-day basis. This post has been rewritten against Anthropic's current [pricing page](https://claude.com/pricing) and help center.

## The plans at a glance

Anthropic sells three paid tiers of Claude for individuals. Limits are published as multiples of Pro, not as message counts.

| Plan | Price | 5-hour limit | Weekly limit | Fable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pro | $20/month ($17/month billed annually) | 1x | 1x | Usage credits only |
| Max | $100/month | 5x Pro | 5x Pro | Up to 50% of weekly limit |
| Ultra (formerly Max 20x) | $200/month | None (verified accounts) | 10x Pro | Up to 50% of weekly limit |

Older guides, including the first version of this post, quoted "~45 / ~225 / ~900 messages per 5 hours." Anthropic doesn't publish message counts, and the help center is explicit about why: how fast you reach a limit depends on the model, the effort level, how long your chats are, and how large your files are. The multiples are the only official numbers.

Two changes are easy to miss. Ultra is not "4x Max": per week it is 2x Max (10x vs. 5x Pro). What you're really buying is the removal of the 5-hour limit. And Max and Ultra now include monthly API credits for the Claude Platform ($100 and $200 respectively), on top of priority access at high-traffic times.

Your usage across claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Desktop all counts against the same limits.

## The 5-hour window

Claude doesn't reset session usage at midnight or on any fixed clock. On Pro and Max, the five-hour period starts when you send your first message, and your limit resets in full five hours later. Send a message at 8:14 AM and the window resets at 1:14 PM. You can see the time remaining under **Current session** in [Settings > Usage](https://claude.ai/settings/usage).

This matters more than people expect. Two users on the same plan can have very different days depending on when they happen to send their first message. If yours consistently lands right before a focused work block, you end up fighting your own window. The mechanics are covered in detail in [How Claude's 5-Hour Session Window Actually Works](/posts/how-claude-5-hour-session-window-works).

Ultra is the exception. Verified Ultra accounts have no 5-hour limit and work only against the weekly limit. Anthropic verifies accounts automatically from account and payment history; until then a temporary 5-hour limit applies. If you don't see a Current session bar in Settings > Usage, your account is verified.

## The weekly limits

Every paid plan also has a weekly limit that applies across all models. The detail most older write-ups get wrong: it does **not** roll over a trailing 7 days. Each account is assigned a fixed reset day and time, and it stays the same regardless of when you start using Claude or when you subscribed. You get the full weekly allowance each cycle, and the next reset is shown in Settings > Usage.

Max and Ultra add a second, separate weekly limit for **Fable**, Anthropic's top model. Fable can use up to half of your weekly limit. On Pro, Fable isn't part of the included usage at all and runs on usage credits.

So a Max or Ultra subscriber who leans on Fable can hit the Fable cap with half the weekly budget left, and has to finish the week on Opus, Sonnet, or Haiku. Earlier versions of this post described the same thing as an Opus cap; Opus is now covered by the all-models limit.

## What this actually feels like day to day

Pro handles casual, spread-out use fine: a few questions across the day, drafting an email, reviewing a short document. Put Pro through a real coding session and it changes fast. Claude Code and long agentic tasks with large files consume the window far quicker than chat, because each turn re-sends a large context.

Max fixes the immediate problem for most people, but limits don't disappear. A recurring forum question is "how did I hit my weekly limit in a few days?", usually from someone who assumed the 5-hour limit was the only one.

Comparing plans by what they actually hold, using the published numbers as multiples of Pro:

| Plan | 5-hour size | Weekly size |
|---|---|---|
| Pro | 1 | 1 |
| Max | 5 | 5 |
| Ultra | — (no 5-hour limit) | 10 |

The same view makes one practical point clear: **unused quota does not carry over.** Whatever is left in a 5-hour window when it resets, or in a week when it resets, is gone. Planning around when windows reset is worth as much as the size of the window.

## Choosing between them

**Stay on Pro if** you rarely hit limits, you're fine on Opus and Sonnet, and your usage is spread across the day rather than packed into long sessions. If you want Fable occasionally, Pro can pay for it with usage credits.

**Upgrade to Max if** you regularly hit the Pro 5-hour or weekly limit, use Claude Code daily, or want Fable included. Max is the plan where the 5-hour window is still the thing you'll feel most.

**Upgrade to Ultra if** the 5-hour limit is what keeps stopping you: long agentic runs, background tasks that go for hours, or days where you need to work without breaks. Ultra doubles Max's weekly budget, but the bigger change is removing the session limit entirely.

If you work in an organization, Team seats are a separate track: a Standard seat gives more usage than Pro, and a Premium seat gives 5x a Standard seat.

## Timing: what upgrading does and doesn't fix

On Pro and Max, upgrading solves a *budget* problem, not a *timing* problem. Both run on the same 5-hour window. If your first message of the day lands at 10 AM, your reset lands at 3 PM on either plan. The plan changes how much fits in a window, not when the window starts.

Controlling that timing means triggering your first call at a moment you choose, rather than whenever your first real message happens. [TokFresh](https://github.com/stevejkang/tokfresh) automates this: a Cloudflare Worker on your own account pre-triggers your usage cycle on a schedule, so the reset lands before your workday instead of in the middle of it.

Verified Ultra accounts don't need this, since there's no 5-hour window to align. The weekly reset can't be shifted on any plan: it's fixed per account.

## What to do when you actually hit a limit

**Wait for the reset.** The 5-hour window refreshes on its own; the weekly limit resets at your account's fixed weekly time. Both are shown in Settings > Usage.

**Use a limit reset if you have one.** Anthropic sometimes grants limit resets, which restore your 5-hour session limit or weekly limits to full.

**Enable usage credits.** All paid plans can turn on usage credits, so work continues on metered credits instead of stopping when included usage runs out.

**Switch to a lighter model or lower effort.** Moving from Fable to Opus, Opus to Sonnet, or lowering the effort level for routine work stretches the remaining budget. Turning off connectors and tools you don't need also helps, since they're token-heavy.

**Upgrade.** If you hit the same wall every week, Max or Ultra fixes the cause. An upgrade takes effect immediately.

Most people's real answer isn't one of these alone. It's the right plan for their volume, plus control over when their windows start.
